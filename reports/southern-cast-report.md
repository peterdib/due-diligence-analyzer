# Due Diligence Report — Southern Cast, Inc.
### Iron & Ductile Casting Foundry — Draft v1, 2026-08-20

*Full source data: `data/processed/southern-cast/key_facts.md`. Raw documents:
`data/raw/southern-cast/`.*

## Bottom line

Southern Cast is an established foundry with real, multi-year revenue ($3.3M–$4.6M/year) and a
history of thin-but-positive net margins (0.6–2.8%) — until **FY2025, where revenue dropped ~21%
and the company posted a net loss**. The equipment base is heavily depreciated (net book value
down to ~$20,000 across all fixed assets by Dec 2025), suggesting real near-term capex exposure
for a capital-intensive foundry operation. There's also an unexplained, unresolved "Fraudulent
Activity" receivable that's sat on the books for two years, and management compensation more
than doubled as a share of revenue in the same year the business lost money. None of this is
disqualifying on its own, but all of it needs direct answers before pricing this deal — and this
analysis is based on financial statements alone; no listing, LOI, contracts, lease, or equipment
data has been reviewed yet.

## What's attractive

- **Established, multi-year operating history** with consistent revenue in the $3.3M–$4.6M range
  across four fiscal years — this is not a startup.
- **Gross margin has trended up**, from 42.2% (FY2022) to 48.6% (FY2025), even as revenue fell in
  FY2025 — suggests some pricing power or cost discipline at the gross-margin line even in a
  weaker revenue year.
- **Diversified product lines** within the casting business (iron castings, ductile castings,
  patterns, purchased castings), not a single-product dependency.
- **Manageable liability trend**: total liabilities have declined every year shown (FY2022
  $1,113,918.64 → FY2025 $798,380.51), and the revolving line of credit balance hasn't grown.

## What's concerning

1. **FY2025 revenue dropped ~21% and the company swung to a net loss.** Revenue fell from
   $4,189,846.66 (FY2024) to $3,309,198.72 (FY2025), and net income went from +$115,950.52 to
   -$60,616.00. This is the single biggest open question in the deal — need to know whether this
   is a lost customer/contract, a capacity/equipment issue, a broader demand slowdown, or
   something else, and whether it's continuing into 2026.
2. **Equipment base is nearly fully depreciated.** Net fixed assets fell from $492,619.97
   (FY2022) to $19,959.00 (Dec 2025) — accumulated depreciation on machinery & equipment and
   vehicles essentially offsets their gross cost. For a foundry, this points to likely near-term
   capital expenditure needs (melting, molding, casting, machining equipment) that aren't
   visible in trailing P&L. Get an independent equipment condition assessment/appraisal before
   valuing off historical earnings.
3. **Unresolved "Fraudulent Activity" receivable.** A balance-sheet line item literally named
   "1127 · Fraudulent Activity Rec" appeared in FY2023 at $27,002.67, dropped to $12,602.01 by
   FY2024, and has sat unchanged at $12,602.01 through Dec 2025 — over two years without
   resolution, write-off, or explanation in any document provided. Needs a direct answer from
   ownership on what happened and why it's still carried as an asset.
4. **Management compensation ("General Supervision") more than doubled as a share of revenue in
   FY2025** (7.9% in FY2024 → 20.0% in FY2025, $330,114 → $663,035.66) — in the same year revenue
   fell and the company lost money. This materially affects any normalized/add-back EBITDA
   calculation and needs a breakdown of what's actually in that account.
5. **Equity has declined every year** shown (FY2022 $753,775.64 → Dec 2025 $489,438.22), including
   in FY2024 despite that year's $115,950.52 net income — implying owner distributions well in
   excess of earnings in at least one year (FY2022→FY2023 equity dropped ~$262k against $24,929
   and $36,354 net income in those two years combined). Get actual distribution history for
   normalized-earnings purposes.
6. **Line of credit balance is identical to the penny for three straight years** ($405,439.63 in
   FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025) — confirm whether this is a static/interest-only balance not being
   paid down, and get the loan agreement, rate, and maturity/renewal terms.
7. **One-year spike in environmental expense** (FY2023: $20,736.15, a ~14x jump from FY2022's
   $1,496.36, before falling back in FY2024–25) — worth a direct question on whether this was a
   fine, remediation, or a one-time compliance project, plus current environmental permit status.
8. **This analysis covers financial statements only.** No FDD/business listing, LOI, purchase
   agreement, lease, customer concentration data, employee census, equipment list, or
   environmental permit records have been reviewed yet.

## Open questions for ownership / broker

- What specifically drove the ~21% FY2025 revenue decline, and is it continuing into 2026
  (get interim 2026 financials if available)?
- What is the age, condition, and replacement cost of core production equipment (melting, mold,
  core, casting/machining lines), given net book value is down to ~$20,000?
- What happened with the "Fraudulent Activity Rec" account — nature of the loss, current status
  (recovered, in litigation, insurance claim), and why $12,602.01 is still on the books?
- What's the breakdown of the FY2025 "General Supervision" expense ($663,035.66) — base
  salaries, bonuses, or distributions run through payroll — for normalized-earnings purposes?
- What is the full distribution history by year (not just net income), to reconstruct actual
  owner cash extraction versus retained earnings?
- What are the terms (rate, maturity, revolving vs. static) of the $405,439.63 line of credit?
- What drove the FY2023 environmental expense spike, and what is current environmental
  permit/compliance status?
- Who are the top customers and what's the concentration (no customer data provided yet)?

## Recommended next steps

1. Request FY2025 interim/YTD 2026 financials to see whether the revenue decline is continuing,
   stabilizing, or reversing.
2. Get an independent equipment appraisal/condition report given how depreciated the fixed-asset
   base is — this should directly inform any capex reserve in a valuation model.
3. Ask ownership directly about the Fraudulent Activity receivable and the FY2025 compensation
   spike before proceeding further — both are the kind of items that belong in a signed engagement
   letter with a CPA doing a Quality of Earnings (QoE) review, not just this financial-statement
   read.
4. Gather the business listing/LOI, lease, customer list, and any environmental permits — this
   report currently covers financials only.
5. If the deal proceeds, commission a formal Quality of Earnings review given the add-back
   ambiguity (officer comp, distributions) visible even at this level of detail.
