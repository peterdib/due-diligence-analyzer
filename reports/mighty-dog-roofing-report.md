# Due Diligence Report — Mighty Dog Roofing Franchise
### Draft v4 — actual FDD reviewed + territory call, 2026-08-31

*Full source data: `data/processed/mighty-dog-roofing/key_facts.md`. Raw documents:
`data/raw/mighty-dog-roofing/`, including the actual issued FDD and franchisor Unit Economics
workbook, both added 2026-08-31.*

## Bottom line

**This deal changed materially for the worse once the actual FDD arrived, and it is now the
weakest of the three franchises reviewed so far.** The sales deck's headline numbers
($1.47M average revenue, strong industry backdrop, sub-contractor labor model) all check out —
but the FDD adds a story the deck never told: after two years of rapid growth (232 → 403
outlets, 2023–2024), the system **lost 74 net outlets in 2025** (-18.4%) on **100 terminations**
against only 26 new openings, royalty revenue fell 14.4%, franchisor net income fell 59%, the
franchisor's own equity is negative and deepening, and **9 separate lawsuits are pending** — 4
where the franchisor is suing departed franchisees for breach of non-compete (each seeking
$800K–$1.35M), and 5 separate franchisee groups suing the franchisor back for fraudulent
misrepresentation, seeking to unwind their franchise agreements entirely. An independent
franchisee association (AAFD chapter) has also asked to be included in the disclosure document.
None of this is disqualifying on its own — litigation and turnover happen in every franchise
system — but the *combination*, all concentrated in the same single year, describes a system in
apparent distress, not the confident growth story in the sales presentation.

The unit economics themselves, now seen with real quartile data instead of a single average,
confirm the exact skew pattern suspected earlier: system-wide **median** Gross Sales ($963,683)
is 34% below the **average** ($1,468,568), and the bottom quartile (18 of 70 reporting
franchisees) averages just $303,699/year with a low of $34,670 — nowhere near enough to service
the debt/overhead most people take on to buy in.

## What's attractive

- **Large, fragmented, recession-resilient industry**: $51.3B roofing market, top 4 competitors
  hold <5% combined share.
- **Real third-party recognition**: 5 consecutive years as Entrepreneur's #1 Roofing Franchise, a
  fast climb in the independent "Top 100 Roofing Contractor" ranking (#90 in 2022 → #20 in 2024)
  — worth verifying directly, but these are checkable claims.
- **Sub-contractor labor model** limits headcount/payroll/workers-comp exposure.
- **Low mandatory overhead to start**: home-based option, minimal core staffing.
- **The Item 7 investment figures now fully reconcile** against an itemized FDD table — no
  hidden gap, unlike when only the sales deck was available.
- **Top-quartile economics, if achievable, are genuinely strong**: $3.6M average / $3.2M median
  Gross Sales for the top 25% of franchisees, with the top decile also showing the best gross
  margins (50.2%) in the supplementary profitability data.

## What's concerning (in order of severity)

1. **100 terminations in 2025 alone, against a base of 403 outlets — roughly 1 in 4.** This
   followed 15 terminations in 2023 and 36 in 2024. New openings collapsed in parallel (147 → 75
   → 26). This is the clearest, most objective signal that something changed materially for the
   worse in this system during 2025, independent of any sales narrative.
2. **9 pending lawsuits, all filed within roughly the same 8-month window (Aug 2025–Mar 2026).**
   Four are the franchisor suing departed franchisees for breach of restrictive covenants and
   unpaid royalties (each seeking $800K–$1.35M in damages) — all four have counterclaims alleging
   fraudulent inducement. Five separate, apparently unrelated, multi-plaintiff franchisee groups
   (at least 9 distinct entities) are separately suing MDR United alleging they were fraudulently
   or negligently misled and are seeking to rescind their franchise agreements entirely. A
   pattern this concentrated, in both directions, in one calendar year is unusual and worth
   understanding directly from the franchisor before proceeding.
3. **An independent franchisee association (Mighty Dog Franchise Owners, an AAFD chapter) has
   asked to be included in the FDD.** These typically form when a meaningful group of franchisees
   feels it needs organized representation against the franchisor. Worth a direct question to the
   franchisor and, ideally, a direct conversation with MDFO itself.
4. **Franchisor financial health is deteriorating.** MDR United LLC's own royalty revenue (the
   metric that most directly tracks franchisee performance) fell 14.4% in FY2025, net income fell
   59%, and the company has run a negative and worsening members' equity position two years
   running — while still distributing $3.545M to owners in 2025, more than the company earned
   that year. This mirrors the negative-equity pattern found in The Handyman Group's Canadian
   guarantor, but here it's the primary franchisor entity itself.
5. **Median vs. average gap confirmed exactly as suspected.** System median Gross Sales
   ($963,683) is 34% below the average ($1,468,568) that was the only figure in the sales deck.
   The bottom quartile (18 of 70 franchisees) averages $303,699/year, with one franchisee at
   $34,670. The supplementary (unofficial) profitability breakdown suggests bottom performers also
   run much thinner margins (28.6% vs. 50.2% for the top decile) — a double penalty of lower
   revenue and lower margin.
6. **Revenue, not profit, is still the only official disclosure.** Item 19 gives Gross Sales only
   — no royalty, labor, marketing, or overhead is netted out anywhere in the FDD itself. The
   percentile-based profitability table (from the franchisor's internal Unit Economics workbook)
   is directional but carries no stated methodology and doesn't cleanly map to Table 1's official
   quartiles.
7. **No exclusive territory, and the franchisor's Contact Center controls appointment routing**
   even within your own territory — appointments aren't guaranteed to reach you. Also reserves
   National/Regional Account rights to service chain customers in your territory without consent.
8. **Royalty tier breakpoints still not disclosed** — "6% scaling to 2%" with no revenue
   thresholds given anywhere, sales deck or FDD.

**Read across all three:** each deal has a distinct failure mode. The Handyman Group is a
*data-blackout* risk — too young to have any disclosed track record, so you'd be pricing the
deal on pure assumption, with a weak guarantor behind it. RealClean is an *early-stage skew*
risk — real but young, with a visible gap between top and bottom performers in a system that
hasn't proven it can support a full territory base yet. **Mighty Dog Roofing is different in
kind: it's a mature-enough system to have generated a real track record, and that track record
now shows the system actively contracting** — the only one of the three with disclosed
litigation, the only one with a documented one-year mass-termination event, and the only one
where the franchisor's own audited financials show declining royalty revenue and eroding equity
in real time. A young system with no data is a bet on the future; a 5-year system that just lost
a quarter of its outlets in one year is evidence about the present.

## Open questions for the franchisor / broker

**New, highest priority given the FDD findings:**

1. What specifically drove the jump from 36 terminations in 2024 to 100 in 2025? Was this
   concentrated in specific cohorts (e.g., a particular launch vintage, a particular region), or
   system-wide?
2. What prompted the formation of the Mighty Dog Franchise Owners (MDFO) independent association,
   and what are its stated concerns?
3. Can we speak with MDFO directly, separate from any franchisor-provided reference list?
4. What is the substance of the 5 pending franchisee-initiated fraud/rescission suits — are the
   underlying allegations about how the opportunity (including Item 19 figures) was represented
   during the sales process similar across the different plaintiff groups?
5. Why did royalty revenue fall 14.4% and net income fall 59% in FY2025 even as franchise-fee
   revenue rose — is the franchise-fee increase from new sales or from recognizing previously
   deferred fees?
6. Why did MDR United distribute $3.545M to its members in 2025, more than its full-year net
   income, in the same year the system contracted by 74 outlets?

## Recommended next steps

1. **Get a franchise attorney reviewing the Franchise Agreement now** — the termination and
   litigation pattern found here raises the stakes on personal guaranty, non-compete, and
   termination-for-cause language well above what a routine review would normally warrant.
2. **Prioritize reference calls with franchisees who left or were terminated in 2025**, not just
   current happy franchisees — the FDD's Exhibit franchisee list should include departed
   franchisees if required by the applicable state's disclosure rules; ask the franchisor
   directly if it's not obvious in the exhibit.
3. **Try to reach the Mighty Dog Franchise Owners (MDFO) association directly** — an independent,
   organized franchisee voice is a rare and valuable source in due diligence.
4. Ask the franchisor to walk through the 2025 termination spike and the 5 pending fraud suits
   directly, and compare their explanation against what the FDD itself discloses.
5. Re-run the Greensboro/Winston-Salem territory comparison once it's clear whether any of the 7
   candidate boundaries overlap a recently vacated territory.
6. Get the underlying methodology for the percentile-based profitability workbook data before
   treating the 50.2%/28.6% gross-margin figures as reliable.
