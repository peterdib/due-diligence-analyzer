# Due Diligence Report — Mighty Dog Roofing Franchise
### Territory options under evaluation — Draft v2, 2026-08-25

**Note on the source document:** the only document gathered so far is a 48-page franchisor
"Discovery Process" sales presentation (HorsePower Brands / Don Schneider, Director of
Franchise Development), not the FDD. It references Item 7 and Item 19 figures but is a sales

## Bottom line

Roofing is a large ($51.3B), fragmented, recession-resilient industry, and Mighty Dog Roofing
comes with real third-party validation (Entrepreneur's #1 Roofing Franchise 5 years running,
rapid Top 100 Roofing Contractor climb) and a sub-contractor labor model that keeps the
franchisee focused on sales/marketing rather than operations. The claimed unit economics look
strong on the surface — $1.47M average revenue, a $6.66M top performer — but this deck gives
revenue only, no cost structure or profit data, and no visibility into how many franchisees were
excluded from the "conforming" average or what the bottom-tier/median franchisee actually looks
like (the same average-vs-median gap that turned out to matter in the RealClean review). The
biggest gap right now isn't a red flag in the deal itself — it's that **no FDD has been obtained
yet**, so there's no Item 19 footnotes, no Item 20 franchisee count/turnover history, no Item 21
franchisor financials, and no actual territory-exclusivity or termination language to review.

## Territory options — Greensboro/Winston-Salem, NC market

which shows already-awarded territories across NC and lets you pull demographic/economic data
for candidate boundaries. Seven "Information Tab Export" files were pulled for candidate
territories in the Greensboro/Winston-Salem area (one territory — Greensboro 5 — was exported
twice, from two different points inside the same boundary, so there are 6 distinct options).

**Read:**

- **Greensboro 3 (Greensboro proper) and Greensboro 8 (Pfafftown) look like the strongest two
  on paper** — both combine high buying power per household (top two), a dense-enough population
  (738 and 1,210/mi² respectively, meaning tighter drive times between jobs than the rural
  options) and a solid older-homeowner base (17.4% and 17.7% age 65+). Greensboro 3 also has by
  far the highest business density (31.3/1,000), which matters given the deck frames commercial
  as an add-on opportunity, not the core play.
- **Greensboro 5 (Colfax/Kernersville) is a reasonable third option** — highest age-65+ share of
  the group (18.1%, more homes at an age where roof replacement becomes likely) and still solid
  buying power, at a lower population density than 3 or 8 (more drive time between jobs).
- **Greensboro 4 (High Point) and Greensboro 6 (Winston-Salem 27107) are weaker on affluence**
  (lowest buying-power-per-household after the two rural options) despite High Point's high
  density — likely a more value-oriented, price-sensitive customer base, which matters given the
  average ticket size ($11,145) claimed in the deck.
- **Greensboro 9 (Walkertown) and the main "NC - Greensboro" (Climax) boundary are the weakest of
  the seven** — both are large, sprawling, low-density (347–358/mi²) rural-fringe territories
  with the lowest buying power per household. A territory this spread out likely means more
  windshield time between jobs for the same housing-unit count as a denser option.
- **All seven exceed the 50,000-home base territory** on total housing units (50,590–67,040) —
  even after backing out renters, several are likely to land above the $59,500 base fee tier
  once actual owner-occupied counts are confirmed. Get the real owner-occupied figure and
  franchisor's actual quoted fee for each specific boundary before treating $59,500 as the
  price.

## What's attractive

- **Large, fragmented, recession-resilient industry**: $51.3B market, top 4 competitors hold
  <5% combined share, and roof repair/replacement is described as a need-not-want expense that
  holds up in downturns.
- **Real, checkable third-party recognition**: 5 consecutive years as Entrepreneur's #1 Roofing
  Franchise, and a fast climb in the independent "Top 100 Roofing Contractor" ranking (#90 in
  2022 → #20 in 2024) — worth verifying directly with Entrepreneur/Roofing Contractor magazine,
  but these are checkable, not just self-reported.
- **Sub-contractor labor model** limits the franchisee's headcount/payroll/workers-comp exposure
  — franchisee runs sales, marketing, and quality control; licensed/insured 1099 subs do the
  installation work.
- **Low mandatory overhead to start**: no office required (can work from home), minimal core
  staffing (GM + 2 sales reps), multiple territories can be run from one location.
- **Technology and lead-gen tools bundled in**: AI drone roof inspection, Hail Trace storm
  tracking, a canvassing/territory app, and an Owens Corning "Curb Appeal Visualizer" — all
  franchisor-provided rather than franchisee-built.
- **Claimed unit economics, if verified, are strong**: $1.47M average franchisee revenue and a
  48% closing ratio would be well above what several other home-service franchises reviewed so
  far have disclosed.

## What's concerning

1. **No FDD reviewed yet.** Everything here comes from a franchisor sales deck. There's no
   Item 20 table (actual outlet counts, opens, closures, transfers, terminations by year), no
   Item 3/4 litigation or bankruptcy history, no Item 21 franchisor financial statements, and no
   Item 12 territory-exclusivity or Item 17 termination/renewal language — all of which have
   surfaced material red flags in every other deal reviewed so far (RealClean, The Handyman
   Group).
2. **Item 19 figures are averages only, from an unspecified "conforming" subset.** The deck
   doesn't say how many of the 80+ franchisees were excluded as non-conforming, doesn't give a
   median or bottom-quartile figure, and doesn't break results down by territory age. A
   $6,658,555 top performer pulling the average up is exactly the kind of skew that made
   RealClean's headline average look better than its bottom-tier cohort actually performed.
3. **Revenue, not profit.** $1.47M average revenue with a 6%-to-2% royalty, sub-contractor labor
   costs, marketing spend, and vehicle/tech overhead could still land anywhere on the
   profitability spectrum — this deck contains zero cost, margin, or owner-benefit data.
4. **Investment range doesn't reconcile.** The itemized "largest expenses" ($59,500 fee +
   ~$17,670 vehicle + $20,000 advertising + $40,000 working capital ≈ $137,170 at the high end)
   fall well short of the stated $173,672–$225,816 total range — the gap (~$36K–$89K) isn't
   itemized anywhere in this deck. Need the full FDD Item 7 table to know what's actually in it
   (insurance, signage, IT/software, additional working capital, etc.).
5. **HorsePower Brands is a multi-brand platform.** Worth understanding how much of the
   corporate support infrastructure, franchisor attention, and marketing budget is shared across
   ~15 different brands versus dedicated to Mighty Dog Roofing specifically, and whether
   HorsePower Brands' own financial health (not disclosed here) has any bearing on Mighty Dog
   Roofing franchisees.
6. **Royalty scaling thresholds not disclosed.** "6% scaling down to 2%" sounds attractive, but
   without the actual revenue breakpoints it's impossible to model what royalty rate a typical
   franchisee actually pays.
7. **Optional supplement-recovery fee structure** ($75–$200/claim) is franchisor-run — worth
   understanding whether this is a genuine value-add or an additional revenue stream for the
   franchisor layered on top of the royalty.

These are back-of-envelope calculations from the Item 19 numbers in the deck, to have concrete
numbers ready for Friday rather than just "$1.47M average revenue." **All are estimates — verify
against the actual FDD and against what the franchisor/broker says on the call.**

|---|---|---|---|

| Jobs/month | ~11 | ~27 | ~50 |

| Leads needed/month | ~23 | ~56 | ~104 |

- The top performer's implied ~50 jobs/month and ~104 leads/month is a very high volume for a
  single sales team — this almost certainly reflects a multi-territory operator (recall the deck
  claims an average of 3.1 territories per franchisee and "several multi-Horsepower-Brand
  franchisees"), not a single-territory result. Worth asking directly whether the top performer
  and top-25% figures are single-territory or blended across multiple territories/brands.
- **Item 7 investment gap, quantified:** the itemized "largest expenses" sum to $114,866 (low) –
  $137,170 (high), against a stated total range of $173,672 – $225,816. That leaves **$58,806 –
  $88,646 (roughly 34%–39% of the total investment) unitemized** in this deck — likely
  insurance, signage/vehicle wrap, computer/software, licenses/permits, training travel, and
  additional working capital, but this needs the actual FDD Item 7 table to confirm.
- **Franchise fee scales down per household as territory size increases**: $1.19/home at 50,000
  homes down to $0.778/home at 250,000 homes (a ~35% discount per household at the largest tier).
  Since royalty stays a flat percentage of revenue regardless of territory size, buying a larger
  territory upfront is cheaper per household on the fee alone but doesn't obviously change the
  ongoing royalty burden — worth understanding whether revenue scales linearly with territory
  size or whether larger territories take proportionally longer to saturate.
