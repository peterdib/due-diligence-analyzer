# Due Diligence Report — The Handyman Group Franchise
### No territory selected yet — Draft v1, 2026-08-25

`data/raw/the-handyman-group/`.*

**Note on the source document:** the FDD PDF is watermarked "IN-PROGRESS / BoldSign Document
ID" on every page — it appears to be an unsigned draft pulled from an e-signature workflow, not
a final issued disclosure document. Confirm you have the actual issued FDD (with an Issuance

## Bottom line

The handyman/home-repair space is a solid, recession-resilient category, and the fee structure
and initial investment ($83K–$131K) are modest compared to most home-service franchises. But
this is an extremely young system — the US franchisor entity is under 18 months old, has grown
from 1 to roughly ~49 sold territories almost entirely in 2025, and **discloses no financial
performance representation at all (Item 19)**. The bigger flag is Item 21: the Canadian
predecessor company that guarantees the US franchisor's obligations is running a **widening
net loss and a $(1.2M) negative equity position** — the entity backstopping the franchisor's
promises is itself in a weak financial state. Territory exclusivity, data-access, and personal
guaranty terms follow the same pattern seen in other young franchise systems reviewed so far
(e.g. RealClean) and should get the same attorney scrutiny.

## What's attractive

- **Low-ish barrier to entry**: $83K–$131K total initial investment is well below most home-
  service and aviation/detailing-style franchises reviewed so far — no retail lease, home-based
  administrative office permitted where local law allows.
- **Durable, non-cyclical demand**: handyman/repair services aren't dependent on a housing boom
  the way remodeling or new-construction trades can be; ongoing maintenance and small repairs
  are needed regardless of the broader economy.
- **Franchisor-managed marketing stack included in a flat monthly fee**: bookkeeping
  (QuickBooks), Google Ads management, and a home-services CRM (HouseCall Pro) are bundled into
  the $1,500/mo Franchise Support Engine Fee rather than being ad hoc franchisee expenses.
- **Rapid territory sell-through**: ~49 territories sold/opened in under 8 months signals real
  demand from the franchisee side, whatever the reason (aggressive sales, attractive economics,
  or both) — worth understanding which it is.
- **No litigation or bankruptcy history disclosed.**

## What's concerning

1. **No Item 19 financial performance representation whatsoever.** Unlike RealClean (which at
   least disclosed one affiliate location's P&L and franchisee cohort data), this FDD gives you
   zero sales, revenue, or margin data to size the opportunity against the $60K+ fee and $10K/yr
   minimum royalty. You are pricing this deal entirely on your own assumptions.
2. **The system has essentially no operating history.** The FDD's own Item 20 table shows only
   4 outlets as of July 31, 2025, and even the ~49-territory figure from the November 2025
   supplement represents at most a few months of live operation for most locations. No
   franchisee in the US network has a full year of results.
3. **The guarantor behind the franchisor's performance obligations is financially weak.** The
   Local Handyman Group Ltd. (the Canadian predecessor providing the performance guaranty) has
   a **$(1,211,305) total deficit** as of July 31, 2025 (worsening from $(886,145) in FY2024),
   a **$(342,489) net loss** in FY2025 (up from $(189,145) the year before — losses are
   accelerating, not shrinking), and a working capital deficit of $571,223. If the US franchisor
   ever needs the guaranty to make good on an obligation, the guarantor's own balance sheet
   raises real questions about its ability to do so.
4. **The US franchisor's own profitability is fee-driven, not royalty-driven.** Local Handyman
   USA Corp.'s $43,168 net income in its first 4-month stub period came almost entirely (98%)
   from upfront franchise fee income ($201,162 of $205,448 total revenue); royalty income was
   only $2,911. That's expected for a brand-new system, but it means the franchisor's current
   business model depends on continuing to sell new territories, not on existing franchisees
   succeeding and paying royalties — worth watching whether that incentive shows up anywhere
   in how aggressively new territories are being sold.
5. **No exclusive territory**, structurally similar to RealClean: the franchisor reserves broad
   rights to operate outside your territory, acquire competing businesses, run a Corporate
   Accounts program inside your territory without compensating you, and solicit/accept orders
   in your territory through other channels or trademarks.
6. **Franchisor has unrestricted access to your business data** (HouseCall Pro, QuickBooks) —
   "no contractual limits" per the FDD's own language in Item 11.
7. **Personal guaranty required from owners and spouses** — same pattern as RealClean; survives
   even if the franchise entity itself fails.
8. **Aggressive multi-state expansion plan** (36 more franchised outlets projected next fiscal
   year, spread across 7+ new states) for a system this young — worth asking how support,
   training capacity, and territory quality control scale alongside that growth rate.

## Open questions for the franchisor / broker

- Can they provide any Item 19-style financial data — even unaudited, even from a single
  location — now that ~23 outlets have some operating history? If not, why not, given the
  system is old enough (Canada since 2017) to have real unit economics somewhere in the group?
- What is driving the guarantor's (The Local Handyman Group Ltd.) widening net loss and growing
  negative equity, and how does the Guaranty (Exhibit F/H) actually work in practice if it's
  ever called on?
- Of the ~49 territories sold/opened as of November 2025, how many are actually generating
  meaningful revenue vs. still ramping or dormant?
- What specifically is included vs. excluded in the "actual costs incurred" pass-through fees
  (Quality Assurance Audit, Certification Program, Supplier Review) — these have no stated cap?
- Is a specific target territory available for evaluation, or is this still an open-market
  franchise-fee decision without a defined territory?

## Recommended next steps

1. Confirm this is the actual issued FDD (Issuance Date, state filing numbers) and not a
   draft — request the final version directly from the franchisor/broker.
2. Get a franchise attorney to review the Franchise Agreement (Exhibit D) — specifically the
   personal guaranty, the data-access/ownership language in Item 11, the territory-exclusivity
   ("Reserved Rights") language in Item 12, and the Guaranty (Exhibit F/H) backing the
   franchisor's obligations.
3. Push the franchisor for any real unit-economics data, even informally, given the total
   absence of an Item 19 representation.
4. Call several current franchisees — prioritize the earliest 2025 openings (Louisiana, Texas)
   for the most operating history — and ask directly about actual monthly revenue, marketing
   spend beyond the flat Franchise Support Engine Fee, and territory-exclusivity conflicts.
5. If a specific territory becomes available, commission independent local market research
   (population density, existing handyman competition, home-value/age profile) the way was
   done for the RealClean Winston-Salem territory, rather than relying on the franchisor's
   Base Territory population figure alone.
